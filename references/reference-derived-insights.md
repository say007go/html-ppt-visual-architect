# Reference-derived insights

These principles were reverse-engineered from the supplied “金控客户需求理解及规划” HTML deck. They are calibration insights, not instructions embedded in the reference and not layouts to copy mechanically.

## What makes the reference feel human-authored

1. **Conclusion-led titles do real work.** The title is often a full judgment, not a topic label. It lets the audience understand the page before reading details.
2. **The page chooses a relationship-specific visual.** The solution page is a layered system; governance is an organization tree plus permission rails; positioning is a five-role field resting on common platform layers; the first-year plan is a timeline crossed by parallel workstreams.
3. **One primary graphic dominates.** Secondary explanations sit beside or below it, so the page has a visual center rather than a collection of unrelated blocks.
4. **Complexity is managed through nesting.** Strategy, talent, engineering, platform, and technical foundations appear at different structural depths. The page does not flatten all information into equal status.
5. **Side rails preserve the main diagram.** Supporting services, scope boundaries, action permissions, three-year evolution, or validation logic are placed in narrow explanatory zones instead of disrupting the central structure.
6. **The same visual system supports different geometries.** Typography, rules, color, margins, and footer stay consistent while the body changes from stack to tree, timeline, progression, quadrant, table, and comparison.
7. **Red is used as punctuation.** A small seal-red marker or a decisive red node creates rhythm without overwhelming the analytical content.
8. **Small labels are subordinate metadata.** Chips identify attributes, functions, and proof points; they do not replace the primary diagram.
9. **Whitespace is structural.** Open areas separate levels, make connectors legible, and give high-density pages an executive feel.
10. **The deck connects strategy to execution.** Several pages trace a continuous chain from group strategy to talent requirements, platform capability, operational cadence, assessment, and contractual evidence.
11. **Auditability is visible.** Rights, gates, dates, percentages, evidence, and consequences appear in the same visual system as the strategy, which makes the proposal credible and procurement-ready.
12. **The page sequence escalates.** Context leads to diagnosis, then transformation, architecture, governance, execution, supplier proof, and commitment. This is a persuasive storyline, not a loose gallery.

## Where the new skill should go further

- Add a distinct lead paragraph beneath every title; the reference often uses the title itself as the summary.
- Increase the share of diagrams that use continuous fields, axes, connectors, brackets, paths, and nested layers, so card-like containers do not dominate.
- Make deck-level layout variety an explicit planning and QA criterion.
- Use a deterministic HTML QA layer to catch overflow, unreadable text, missing leads, duplicate layout signatures, and excessive card use.
- Shift the full system to red while preserving the reference's restraint and neutral reading surfaces.
- Keep all diagrams editable in HTML/CSS/SVG and make offline behavior a release requirement.

## Lessons from the five-page forward test

- The project-objectives page succeeded because its rising spatial structure reinforced the four-step value chain and used the body with confidence. This is evidence for semantic fit and spatial allocation, not a reason to standardize ascending diagrams.
- The design-approach page had a sound closed-loop idea but weak execution: approximate radial placement, irregular repetition, scattered explanatory blocks, and unused central space. The correction is not a ring-specific template. It is a general rule that repeated geometry must come from a shared coordinate system and that page area must be budgeted from content weight.
- The test deck removed too much source detail. Future work must establish a content ledger before visual design and treat unique source information as preserved by default.
- High information density should be achieved through compression, grouping, annotation, and efficient carriers. It should not be mistaken for either tiny text or a wall of equal cards.

## Governance-page lesson: integration through responsibility, not proximity

The governance page is a strong example of `diagram-led` coupling rather than deep embedding:

- the dominant graphic owns only the structural topology: headquarters, branch academies, horizontal business lines, delegation, and shared control;
- two larger interpretation zones separately explain the scope dimension and action dimension;
- rules, switches, temporary authorization, and log retention remain outside the architecture nodes because they do not change the topology;
- the graphic and text are visually separated but semantically joined by the same governance thesis;
- the asymmetric graphic/interpretation ratio creates hierarchy and prevents a wall of equal modules;
- the two explanation areas behave as dimensions of one framework, not as independent cards.

The transferable rule is not “place a diagram on the left and two cards on the right.” It is:

`structural topology → core graphic; shared operating dimensions → independent interpretation; evidence and conditions → attached detail`

Use this logic whenever embedding all source copy would deform a clear framework. Keep integrated pages when details genuinely belong to individual nodes, so the deck alternates coupling strategies rather than replacing one default with another.

The governance graphic also demonstrates multi-grammar synthesis. Its primary grammar is vertical delegation from headquarters to branch academies; a secondary horizontal grammar lets business lines penetrate the same branches; two lateral capability anchors explain what headquarters provides. These relations share targets but not direction, so flattening them into one tree or matrix would lose meaning. The figure can be derived by assigning each semantic dimension its own visual variable and then composing them around one primary skeleton. This is not a reason to standardize governance diagrams or topology-led pages.

## Transferable value

The central transferable asset is not a look; it is a reasoning chain:

`page meaning → perceptual task → visual variables → primary and secondary grammar → custom composition → evidence placement → exact geometry → rendered QA`

That chain allows different agents to produce varied pages with a stable quality floor without relying on rigid templates.
